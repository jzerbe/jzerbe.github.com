__Result:__ Run the household's money through a business entity in a low tax jurisdiction, and pay income tax where the money is made.
In the US, Wyoming wins if you want no taxes, and Colorado wins if you want low taxes with good services.
In Canada, I registered in British Columbia with a location in Alberta, which gets the low fees and automation of both.

## The Problem
> In any situation, location is crucial.
>
_Gordon Ramsay_

Taxes stack up at every layer of where you work: city, county, region, province, and country.
When people rarely moved (even across town), their work touched their neighbors and that was about it.
Now my work can touch someone who is not even on this planet.
Of course [astronauts have Internet access](https://usa.kaspersky.com/blog/internet-in-space/18618/)!

If I spend most of my time in one place (the Denver metro area), residency is easy.
But what if I split my time between a coworking space in Calgary, a townhome in Buenos Aires, and a cabin in Pagosa Springs?
Each place has its own rules for what work gets taxed and how much.

It gets messier when the means of production (solar panels, land, crops) are managed from thousands of miles away.
I do some of the work from my cabin, sure.
But which government gets the taxes on electricity my solar panels sell to a utility in another state?

## The Approach
[Tax arbitrage](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/t/tax-arbitrage.asp):
profiting from differences in how income and transactions are taxed in different places.
Business entities can be created in any jurisdiction you want, and they are usually taxed at lower rates.

The vehicle is a family office:

> From investment management to charitable giving advice, family offices offer a total financial solution.
> In addition, the family office can also handle non-financial issues, such as private schooling,
> travel arrangements, and miscellaneous other household arrangements.
>
What Are Family Offices? <https://www.investopedia.com/terms/f/family-offices.asp>

You run the family office and you are also the beneficiary of its services.
That matters later for deductions.
The family office will not be selling anything, but it will be earning plenty of income.
So I compared places on two things: lowest income taxes, and easy to do business in
(reasonable filing fees, online services, responsive officials).

## US Territories
Most US territories offer heavy tax incentives for businesses (and people) to move to their islands.
You pay the [territory treasury and never the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/individuals-living-or-working-in-us-possessions).

The catch: [beware if you still live in a US state](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/957).
You get hit with extra penalties for owning or getting income from a
"[controlled foreign corporation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/subtitle-A/chapter-1/subchapter-N/part-III/subpart-F)",
so you would do just as well with a "foreign corporation" in a truly zero tax foreign location.
Spotty internet and irregular flights also make a business hard to run remotely.

- __Puerto Rico__: 4% tax rate for export companies.
  - <https://www.ddec.pr.gov/en/puerto-rico-incentives-code/>
  - <https://nomadcapitalist.com/2018/06/02/puerto-rico-tax-incentives/>
- __Guam__: 75% rebate for certain industries, if you invest at least $10,000. <https://www.investguam.com/business-in-guam/investor-incentives/>
- __US Virgin Islands__: 90% income tax rebate if you employ 10 people and invest $100,000. <https://www.usvieda.org/incentives/edc-program>

## US States
Some states have no or low sales tax. Others have no or low income tax. For a family office, income tax is what counts.

__No income tax:__ Only South Dakota and Wyoming levy no corporate income tax, no individual income tax, and no
[gross receipts tax](https://taxfoundation.org/gross-receipts-tax/).

- [South Dakota](https://sdsos.gov/general-information/filing-fees.aspx) has some of the highest filing fees I have seen outside of
[Nevada](https://www.nvsos.gov/sos/businesses/commercial-recordings/forms-fees).
- [Wyoming's fees are on the higher side](https://sos.wyo.gov/Business/docs/BusinessFees.pdf), but you are paying for lower taxes, right?
- Both have easy, responsive secretary of state websites for online filing.

__Watch out for gross receipts tax states.__ They tend to have business licenses at the state, county, and local level too.
Nevada is the prime example: a few hundred dollars at just about every level of government adds up.
Wyoming has no state business license fee, no annual list filing fee, and very few local business licenses,
usually only for certain controlled industries.

__Flat income tax under 5%:__ Colorado, Florida, North Dakota, and Utah.

- Florida: skipped, since it will be underwater in 20 years.
- North Dakota: [online filing fees](https://sos.nd.gov/business/business-services/business-structures/limited-liability-companies/limited-liability-company-llc/llc-fees.html) on the higher side.
- Utah: [slightly higher fees](https://corporations.utah.gov/pdf/currentfees.pdf) than [Colorado's](https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/info_center/fees/business.html).

## Canada
Every Canadian province and territory has income tax and sales tax.
There are still ways to optimize across income tax, investment goals, family needs, and how much you want to do yourself.
But if saving a percent or two means a real pain managing the legal requirements, is it worth it?
And why base a business somewhere that will be less stable because of climate change?
For my risk and reward profile, interior British Columbia made the most sense for my headquarters.

__Sales tax:__ In [Alberta, Yukon, Nunavut, and the Northwest Territories, you only pay the 5% federal GST](https://borderbuddy.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-gst-hst-and-pst/).
Buy your goods there.

<img src="/assets/posts/map-canada-01.png.webp" alt="drawing" class="image fit" />

__Corporate income tax:__ Canada allows fewer loopholes for passive income than most places.
Investment income is taxed at a much higher national rate, and it is defined as income from property:

> A specified investment business is carried on by a corporation whose principal purpose is to derive
> income (interest, rent, dividends and royalties) from property, unless the business employs more than
> 5 full time employees. Income from property would include rental or leasing income from land or buildings,
> but would exclude income from renting or leasing moveable property such as machinery and equipment.
>
<https://accountingottawa.com/active-business-income-small-business-deduction/>

<img src="/assets/posts/canada_2020_corporate_income_tax_rates.png" alt="drawing" class="image fit" />

From <https://www.taxtips.ca/smallbusiness/corporatetax/corporate-tax-rates-2020.htm>

You also cannot shield passive income by bundling it into an entity that earns some or most of its income actively
([unless you have, or could employ, 5 employees](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4012/t2-corporation-income-tax-guide-chapter-4-page-4-t2-return.html#P2883_209821)).

__Personal income tax:__ A corporation can retain earnings, which keeps your personal rate low
(only pay yourself dividends when you need the cash).
The corporation can also be taxed in a different jurisdiction than you are.
Like corporate rates, the territories have the lowest personal rates:
<https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/frequently-asked-questions-individuals/canadian-income-tax-rates-individuals-current-previous-years.html#provincial>

__Verdict:__

- __Alberta__: for the lowest income taxes and other "business friendly" choices, including
[letting foreigners run their own company](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/canadian-securities-regulatory-monitor/alberta-eases-director-residency-requirements).
DIYers beware: you cannot file directly, you have to go through a service provider. <https://www.alberta.ca/incorporate-alberta-corporation.aspx>
- __British Columbia__: for a balance of lower taxes, better services,
[relaxed director requirements](https://www.canadianbusinessresources.ca/qualifications-of-directors-of-british-columbia-companies/),
and [automated services](https://www.bcregistry.ca/).

__What I did:__ An entity domiciled and registered in British Columbia
([350CAD to register, 45CAD to renew](https://www.corporateonline.gov.bc.ca/WebHelp/fee_schedule.htm)),
with a location in Alberta too.
Alberta has cheap, automated (14CAD) [online registration](https://newwestregistration.alberta.ca/) for businesses under the
[New West Partnership Trade Agreement](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/permits-licences/businesses-incorporated-companies/new-west-partnership-trade-agreement).
You pay income tax where the money is made.
Registering directly in Alberta costs
[300 to 500CAD to register and 70 to 90CAD a year](https://www.ownr.co/blog/how-to-incorporate-in-alberta/)
to renew, depending on the service provider.

Use [BizPal](https://www.bizpal.ca/) for a full list of licenses you may need.

## Outside North America
Serious international tax arbitrage is out of scope here. A few tips in the meantime.

For the Canada Revenue Agency:

- If you file with the CRA as a Canadian domestic entity and hold $100,000 CAD or more in foreign assets, you have to file Form T1135: <https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/foreign-reporting/foreign-income-verification-statement.html>
- Canada has income tax treaties to prevent double taxation: <https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/programs/tax-policy/tax-treaties.html>

For the US Internal Revenue Service:

- If you have signature authority over more than $10,000 USD in foreign financial institutions, as owner or trustee, you have to file FBAR: <https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts>
- If you file with the IRS as a US domestic entity and hold $50,000 USD or more in foreign financial assets, you have to file Form 8938: <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/corporations/do-i-need-to-file-form-8938-statement-of-specified-foreign-financial-assets>
- The US has income tax treaties with other countries, so you will not necessarily be taxed twice on the same income: <https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z>

## How to Use the Lower Rate
Forming an entity somewhere else does not magically move all your income to the lower rate. It is not hard, though. At a high level:

1. Get a street address in the other location with [Anytime Mailbox]({{ site.program.business.anytimemb }}) or [iPostal1]({{ site.program.business.ipostal1 }}).
Use it for your business address and [registered agent address](https://howtostartanllc.com/what-is-a-registered-agent).
Plenty of companies will also manage all of this for higher "all inclusive" fees.
2. Create a holding LLC in the low tax jurisdiction. LLCs usually have lower filing fees and less paperwork than C-corporations,
and you do not
[pay tax on both the entity's earnings and what you pay yourself](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/s-corp-vs-c-corp-differences-benefits).
3. Transfer ownership of all assets (intellectual, tangible, or otherwise) from your existing business to the new one.
4. Have the existing business pay significant rent or royalties to the holding company, which wipes out the existing company's tax burden.
Doing this across borders? Look at the [Multi Currency Account from Wise]({{ site.program.business.wise }}).
5. [Elect to have the holding LLC taxed as a corporation](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/llc-filing-as-a-corporation-or-partnership)
so the profits stay in the low tax jurisdiction.
Beware: this can set you up for
[personal holding company penalties](https://www.henssler.com/avoiding-personal-holding-company-tax/https://www.henssler.com/avoiding-personal-holding-company-tax/).
It may be easier to spend most of the revenue on an
[American Express company card]({{ site.program.card.amex }}) and let the remaining profit pass through.

For a deeper Wyoming example, read: <https://www.northwestregisteredagent.com/start-a-business/wyoming/tax-benefits>

## Business Deductions
Once the household is a business (a family office), managing the family's wealth is business activity.
That opens up deductions for keeping your family successful, strong, healthy, and wealthy:

- Legal fees for creating the family business
- Your home office and virtual office
- Telecom services
- Paying employees (family members)
- Interest on purchases that grow the family's wealth
- Insurance
- Family meetings

The list goes on:

- <https://turbotax.intuit.com/tax-tips/small-business-taxes/taking-business-tax-deductions/L5RueYPVS>
- <https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/money-finance/manage-finances/tax-deductible-expenses>

Remember you need to show a profit in at least 3 of the last 5 years: <https://www.thebalancemoney.com/is-this-business-for-real-or-is-it-a-hobby-397675>
