__Result:__ Ongoing. Ask me a year ago and I would have said mixed.
The house worked: built, permitted, and the cozy desert Southwest home I always wanted.
The money did not, at least not yet. Construction came to about 617,000 USD all-in on a 12,500 USD lot,
more than half of it site work and water,
and with a property management company running it, the cash flow came in below what I built it for.
The first year added its own surprise: rotten-egg well water that has taken about 9,100 USD of fixes so far.
So in fall 2026 I am trying something new: managing the rental myself, with AI and LLMs doing most of the work.
My hypothesis is that this gets the cash flow close to where I want it.

## The Solution I Tried

### Pick a county that lets you build
What I wanted:

- Year-round activities: mountain biking, snowboarding, splitboarding, rafting, motorbiking
- Good food and some cultural events
- Affordable
- Driveable from my (at the time) main work location in the foothills around Boulder and Golden

The shortlist: Grand Junction, Gunnison, Hayden, Norwood, Pagosa Springs, Pinedale, Poncha Springs, Sheridan, South Fork, Taos.
I narrowed it to Pagosa Springs and Taos because of their libertarian approach to housing:
I could build what I wanted, how and when I wanted, with less paperwork than most places.
Creede in Mineral County was another interesting spot.
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
Some Colorado counties, Mineral, Baca, Sedgwick, Delta, and Montezuma among them, have
<a href="https://doh.colorado.gov/jurisdictions-without-building-departments-hud-code-homes">no building department at all</a>.
The state's new wildfire resiliency code (SB23-166) reaches wildland-urban interface areas even there.
<a href="https://ccionline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CCI-1225-conf-CO-state-mandated-codes-for-locals.pdf">CCI summary</a>
</span>

I picked unincorporated Archuleta County (Aspen Springs 3), just west of Pagosa Springs:

- Close to tourist attractions, which drives occupancy
- Good food
- _Many_ mountain biking options
- Mild climate with quick access to big, snowy mountains
- Driveable for a long weekend

The catch: high fire risk and very few property insurance options.

### Permits and excavation
Reading [Archuleta County's Modular building permit](https://www.archuletacounty.gov/county-government/development-services/building-department/permit-applications/)
in 2022 wasn't too painful, even before consumer-grade LLMs took off. What the packet asked for:

1. Complete Building Permit Application: _easy, just contact details_
2. 11X17 Proposed Site Plan by a Colorado Licensed Surveyor: _a few months and a few grand with an engineering firm_
3. (2) Sets of Plans: _the rural county wanted paper_ <https://theplanprinter.com/>
4. [Access Approval & Right of Way Permits](https://www.archuletacounty.gov/county-government/public-works/county-engineer/right-of-way-permits/):
pay the fee, bore under the road for electrical, build a driveway over a metal culvert, pass the final inspection
5. Sanitation: design the septic system with an engineering firm like [Davis Engineering](https://www.daveng.com/),
build it with an excavation team like [Pagosa Landworks](https://www.pagosaequipment.co/),
then get the final review once it is powered with a warning alarm
6. [Pagosa Fire Protection District](https://www.pagosafire.org/impact-fee-schedule) impact fee
7. Proof of Ownership: _already had this from closing_
8. HOA or POA approval: _N/A for Aspen Springs, they only maintain the dirt roads_
9. Pay the permit fee (cash or check only)

Pagosa Landworks took massive amounts of material out of the hillside, and we did some extremely creative things with Davis Engineering.
You could probably cut the excavation cost in half on a flat lot.

### Water
By this point I had become much more averse to novel construction.
I had planned a [rain water collection system](https://www.alltexirrigation.com/rain-harvesting) with a massive underground cistern.
After some confident words from [DAK Drilling and Well Services](https://dakdrilling.com/),
I went with what most people in the area do: drill a well.

The San Juan River basin is already overtapped, and it is part of the Upper Colorado River Basin,
where USGS projects groundwater flow to streams
[could drop 29% by the 2050s](https://www.usgs.gov/news/state-news-release/groundwater-flow-colorado-river-may-decline-third-over-next-30-years)
in a hot, dry scenario.
My guess is 5-10 good years out of the well, then another 5-10 mediocre years with the very large pressure tank I put in.
That gives me lead time to add a proper cistern later.
The only legal use for this well water is inside domestic use (showering, drinking, washing), then back into the ground through the septic system.

Just drilling the well to ~500ft was 52,050 USD. They make you sign a contract saying they may never hit water.
A flat lot closer to surface water would probably take 25-50% off the drilling price.
All in, the well, pump, pressure tank, treatment system, and well house came to 125,476 USD.
Still way better than having water delivered like some of the neighbors.

### Electricity
Electricity was far simpler than water.
The power pole was across the dirt road, and we put the house as close to the road as possible.
That kept down the cost of the driveway, the slope of the driveway (code has a maximum), and the cost of running power to the lot disconnect.

[Alpine Excavation](https://www.facebook.com/alpineexcavationpagosa/) bored horizontally under Echo Ln to the power pole on the other side
and fed conduit through the hole. Much cheaper and simpler than cutting into the road.
[JBX Electric](https://www.facebook.com/jbxelectric/) put in the main power pedestal disconnect
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
Think something like: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0yc7c_axEy8>
</span>,
fixed some internal wiring once the house was delivered, and wired the septic and wellhouse.

This is the most critical part of any build, and the part I handed to [HYS Construction](https://hysconstructionanddevelopment.com/).
There is no way to do it well remotely with no general contracting experience.

and most construction workers have never seen the modular style we were attempting.

## The First Year After the Certificate of Occupancy
_July 31, 2025 through October 2026._

### The hydrogen sulfide saga: about 9,100 USD so far
The well water smells like rotten eggs. That is hydrogen sulfide (H2S) gas, and it showed up in spring 2025,
right as the house was getting ready for guests.
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
Sulfate-reducing bacteria feed on the sulfate in the water and give off H2S.
My 2024 well test showed high sulfate, a pH of 8.05, and almost no iron (0.083 mg/L).
</span>

- __Spring 2025: the water treatment system (UV and under-sink reverse osmosis), 15,096 USD.__ Installed with the pump and pressure tank. The kitchen tap was great. Every other tap still smelled.
- __August-September 2025: carbon block filters in the pumphouse, 1,296 USD.__ Worked for 5-6 months.
- __February-March 2026: the smell came back.__ Replaced every filter, including the UV and reverse osmosis, for 2,214 USD.
The odor in the house was gone, but the gas at the well was the same. The driller floated a cistern to aerate the water if it got worse.
- __September 2026: back in force.__ The property manager smelled it dropping off supplies.
I put a maintenance hold on the calendar from September 9 to October 28 instead of collecting bad reviews.
- __October 21, 2026: an air injection sulfur filter with an ozone kit, 5,618 USD.__
Air injection oxidizes the H2S before it reaches the pressure tank, so there is no carbon media to foul every 3-6 months.

What I would do differently: get an on-site H2S reading before buying anything.
Nobody ever measured the sulfide level, so every fix was a guess.

### Everything else
- __Leach field repair, April 2026: 1,480 USD.__ A fitting in the original septic install was the wrong type.
- __Mini-split, May-June 2026:__ no heat or cooling in any zone. I reached out to half a dozen HVAC companies before one fixed a leaking line set.
- __Electrical breakers, September 2026:__ breaker problems. The fix is bundled with the EV charger install.
- __Deck, September 2026: about 4,000 USD in labor.__ An 8x8 dining area and a 4x14 walkway out front, in composite, for shoulder season appeal.
- __Level 2 EV charger, September-October 2026: 1,170 USD in hardware.__ Install in progress.
- __Running costs:__ property insurance through the Colorado FAIR Plan at 1,632 USD a year, a 724 USD county vacation rental permit,
a radon test (2.6 pCi/L, no mitigation needed), and snow removal.

## Did It Work?
__The house: yes.__

Fire resistant metal roof and siding, defensible space with all the shale exposed, and a south-facing driveway to keep the snow off.
A _Type II: Noncombustible_ structure in this area made the property insurance premium a lot easier.

<img src="/assets/posts/Echo_Ln_August_2025_2.jpg" alt="Southwest decor inside the container home" class="image fit" />

I always fantasized about a cozy home in the desert Southwest and stumbled into it with the right siting and accents.

__The money: not yet.__ It took over four years from land to Certificate of Occupancy.

|---|---:|
| Land (12,500 USD price plus closing) | 13,650 USD |
| Site work: excavation, septic, driveway, retaining wall, utility connection | 203,205 USD |
| Well, pump, and water treatment | 125,476 USD |
| Building and foundation: the house, foundation, electrical | 272,261 USD |

| __Construction total__ | __617,350 USD__ |

Furnishings are not in there yet. Add a professional manager taking their cut.
Then the first year added about 9,100 USD chasing the smell, a few thousand in repairs, and seven weeks blocked off on the calendar.
The cash flow came in under the number _JZ the business_ needs.

__What I would tell you before you try this:__

- __Flat lots are cheaper.__ The hillside roughly doubled excavation and added 25-50% to the well.
- __Water is the gamble.__ About 125,000 USD, and the well comes with no guarantee of water.
- __Hire a general contractor for the foundation.__ Remote, with no GC experience, I could not have done it.
- __Concrete crews are the bottleneck.__ I lost the whole 2024 building season trying to find reliable concrete workers.
- __Chase the paperwork.__ The final state stamp sat for months waiting on paperwork from the house manufacturer.
- __Test the water for what you are actually fighting.__ Measure H2S on site before picking a treatment.

## What Is Next: AI-Led Management
The same bet I made with [Vraid Systems](/2026/03/running-a-business-with-llms/):
the clerical work is what eats the margin, and LLMs are good at clerical work.
Guest messaging, pricing, and scheduling are mostly clerical work, and that is a big part of what a property manager charges for.
In October 2026 I gave my property manager notice and started running it myself, with AI doing most of the work.

Running it myself also builds toward the hours the IRS wants to see for a rental to count as a real business.
More on that in the [Colorado LLC](/2020/08/colorado-llc/) post.
