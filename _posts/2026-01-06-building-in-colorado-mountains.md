__Result:__ The house is built, permitted, and set up as a Vacasa-managed rental.
It took a little over four years from buying the land (April 2021) to the Certificate of Occupancy (July 2025).
Site work, water, and power alone came to about 244,000 USD, before the foundation and the house itself.
The steep lot and the water were the expensive parts. I got the cozy desert Southwest home I always wanted.

## What I Tried

### Picking the area
What I wanted:

- Year-round activities: mountain biking, snowboarding, splitboarding, rafting, motorbiking
- Good food and some cultural events
- Affordable
- Driveable from my (at the time) main work location in the foothills around Boulder and Golden

The shortlist: Grand Junction, Gunnison, Hayden, Norwood, Pagosa Springs, Pinedale, Poncha Springs, Sheridan, South Fork, Taos.

I narrowed it to Pagosa Springs and Taos because of their libertarian approach to housing.
I could build what I wanted, how and when I wanted, with less paperwork than most places.
Creede in Mineral County was another interesting spot, but soon you will have to do _some_ paperwork there too.
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
Colorado HB23-1240 requires all counties to adopt statewide building codes (2018 or 2021 IBC/IRC) by mid-2026.
Before that, counties like Mineral, Baca, Sedgwick, Delta, and Montezuma had no codes, no building department,
and in some cases no certificate of occupancy requirement.
<https://doh.colorado.gov/jurisdictions-without-building-departments-hud-code-homes>
</span>

I picked unincorporated Archuleta County (Aspen Springs 3), just west of Pagosa Springs. It had the best mix of:

- Close to tourist attractions, which drives occupancy
- Good food
- _Many_ mountain biking options
- Mild climate with quick access to big, snowy mountains
- Driveable for a long weekend

The catch: high fire risk and very few property insurance options.
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
Take a look at <https://co-pub.coloradoforestatlas.org/>!
I could only find commercial coverage through <https://www.coloradofairplan.com/> with a 1,600 USD/year premium.
</span>

### Finding the house
Over the summer and fall of 2021 I went through the

### Planning and excavation
I had the lot and the house. Now for the tedious part: the local rules on where I could build and what paperwork I needed.
Reading [Archuleta County's Modular building permit](https://www.archuletacounty.gov/county-government/development-services/building-department/permit-applications/)
in 2022 wasn't too painful, even before consumer-grade LLMs took off. What the information packet asked for:

1. Complete Building Permit Application (sign and date): _easy, just contact details_
2. 11X17 Proposed Site Plan by a Colorado Licensed Surveyor: _a few months and a few grand with an engineering firm_
3. (2) Sets of Plans, see Plans & Specifications and Plan Requirements Packet: _the rural county wanted paper_ <https://theplanprinter.com/>
4. Access Approval & Right of Way Permits
   1. Get and fill in the right paperwork <https://www.archuletacounty.gov/county-government/public-works/county-engineer/right-of-way-permits/>
   2. Pay the fee to build a driveway and bring in utilities
   3. Do the construction: horizontal bore under the road for electrical, and a driveway over a metal culvert
   4. Get the final inspection
5. Sanitation (Archuleta County Water Quality approved permit if applicable)
   1. Design the septic system with an engineering firm like [Davis Engineering](https://www.daveng.com/).
   2. Get a permit to build it with an excavation team like [Pagosa Landworks](https://www.pagosaequipment.co/).
   3. Once the septic system is powered with a warning alarm, you can get the final review.
6. Pagosa Fire Protection District Receipt: pay them at <https://www.pagosafire.org/impact-fee-schedule>
7. Proof of Ownership: _already had this from closing_
8. Letter of Approval from your Architectural Control Committee, HOA or POA: _N/A for Aspen Springs, they only maintain the dirt roads_
9. ~~Wetland permit if required~~
10. ~~Aviation easement if required~~
11. Pay Permit Fee (cash or check only)

Pagosa Landworks took massive amounts of material out of the hillside, and we did some extremely creative things with Davis Engineering.

__Engineering designs, permits, excavation, and septic: 108,471 USD.__ You could probably cut that in half on a flat lot.

### Gambling on water
By this point I had become much more averse to novel construction.
I had planned a [rain water collection system](https://www.alltexirrigation.com/rain-harvesting) with a massive underground cistern.
After some confident words from [DAK Drilling and Well Services](https://dakdrilling.com/),
I went with what most people in the area do: drill a well.

The San Juan River basin is already overtapped and will keep receding over the coming years.
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
<https://www.usgs.gov/news/state-news-release/groundwater-flow-colorado-river-may-decline-third-over-next-30-years>
</span>
My guess is 5-10 good years out of the well, then another 5-10 mediocre years with the very large pressure tank I put in.
That gives me lead time to add a proper cistern later.
The only legal use for this well water is inside domestic use (showering, drinking, washing), then back into the ground through the septic system.

Just drilling the well to ~500ft was 52,050 USD. They make you sign a contract saying they may never hit water.
A flat lot closer to surface water would probably take 25-50% off the drilling price.

__Total to get potable water on the property: 120,078 USD.__
Still way better than having water delivered like some of the neighbors.

### Bringing in electricity
Electricity was far simpler than water.
The power pole was across the dirt road, and we put the house as close to the road as possible.
That kept down the cost of the driveway, the slope of the driveway (code has a maximum), and the cost of running power to the lot disconnect.

[Alpine Excavation](https://www.facebook.com/alpineexcavationpagosa/) bored horizontally under Echo Ln to the power pole on the other side
and fed conduit through the hole. Much cheaper and simpler than cutting into the road.
[JBX Electric](https://www.facebook.com/jbxelectric/) put in the main power pedestal disconnect
<span class="sidenote-number"></span><span class="sidenote">
Think something like: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0yc7c_axEy8>
</span>,
fixed some internal wiring once the house was delivered, and wired the septic and wellhouse.

__Total electrification cost: 15,289 USD.__

This is the most critical part of any build, and the part I handed to [HYS Construction](https://hysconstructionanddevelopment.com/).
There is no way to do it well remotely with no general contracting experience.

and most construction workers have never seen the modular style we were attempting.

## Lessons

- __Flat lots are cheaper.__ The hillside roughly doubled excavation and added 25-50% to the well.
- __Water is the gamble.__ Over 120,000 USD, and the well comes with no guarantee of water.
- __Hire a general contractor for the foundation.__ Remote, with no GC experience, I could not have done it.
- __Concrete crews are the bottleneck.__ I lost the whole 2024 building season trying to find reliable concrete workers.
- __Chase the paperwork.__ The final state stamp sat for months waiting on paperwork from the house manufacturer.
