__Result:__ My fix is a virtual mailbox that keeps an address in your original work location.
Your employer avoids a compliance headache.

## The Problem
As an employee, you mostly do not deal with government paperwork.
Maybe a few hours at tax time, nothing like what your employer handles.

That changes when you want to work from somewhere else.
You may get notices from your employer like "you may work internationally for up to one month" or
"as long as you stay within your original employment state".
The reason: an employee who moves, or works a long time in a different province, state, or country,
is a big compliance headache for the employer.

If an employer has a permanent employee in a region, it has to create a local entity or use an
[Employer of Record](https://boundlesshq.com/blog/employment/what-is-an-employer-of-record/)
to stay compliant. Because of you.

## What I Suggest
Use a virtual mailbox to keep an address in your original employment location.
That keeps things easy for your employer. Pick one based on how you plan to work remote:

- [Anytime Mailbox]({{ site.program.business.anytimemb }})
- [iPostal1]({{ site.program.business.ipostal1 }})
- A USPS PO Box with [informed delivery](https://www.usps.com/manage/informed-delivery.htm)
plus a [real street address](https://postalpro.usps.com/PBSA)

If you use a US address, make sure it is not flagged as a
[Commercial Mail Receiving Agency](https://www.smarty.com/docs/cmra).
Check it with the [USPS address lookup tool](https://tools.usps.com/zip-code-lookup.htm?byaddress).
